# RAM & NVMe Storage

System memory (RAM) acts as ultra-fast workspace for active game data, while NVMe SSDs (Solid State Drives) store your operating system and games for near-instant load times.

## RAM Speed, Latency & Capacity
For DDR5 gaming builds, **32GB (2x16GB)** at **6000MT/s** with **CL30 or CL32** latency hits the sweet spot for performance and stability. Always buy a **two-stick kit** instead of four, since running four DDR5 sticks strains the memory controller on your [[cpu-motherboard-pairing|CPU]] and often forces slower speeds. Also check the height of tall RGB heat spreaders if you plan on using a large dual-tower air cooler for [[pc-cooling-airflow|cooling]].
### Dual-Channel & XMP / EXPO Profiles
* **Dual-Channel Kits:** Buying a matched two-stick kit lets your motherboard run memory in dual-channel mode (slots A2 and B2 on standard four-slot boards) for full bandwidth.
* **XMP / EXPO Ready:** New DDR5 kits default to slow baseline speeds (like 4800MT/s) out of the box. Look for kits rated for **EXPO** (AMD) or **XMP** (Intel) so you can unlock their advertised speed with one toggle in the BIOS.

## NVMe Generations & Storage Tiering
A quality **PCIe Gen4 NVMe SSD** (~7,000 MB/s) loads games nearly identically to a much pricier Gen5 drive, making Gen4 the best value for gaming. Place your primary drive in the **top M.2 slot** closest to the processor so it uses direct CPU lanes rather than slower chipset lanes, and remember to peel the protective plastic film off your motherboard's M.2 heatsink pad before screwing it down.

If your budget allows, split your storage across two drives: put Windows and everyday apps on a **1TB primary SSD** and dedicate a separate **2TB or 4TB SSD** to your games so a future Windows reset never wipes your game library.
